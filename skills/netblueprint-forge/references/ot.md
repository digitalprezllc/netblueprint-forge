# OT cards: controllers, HMIs, historians and the conduits between zones

Cards for operational technology, industrial control and building systems, in the same format as the other cards. Use one the moment a node is "the PLC", "the SCADA server", "the historian", "the vendor's remote access"; run its questions, propose with `suggested: true` and `suggested_from: "<card id>"`, and pre-fill its threats for the user to confirm.

Model shape follows IEC 62443: each OT network is a zone of kind `ot` with a `security_level` (the target level, SL-T 1 to 4); a flow between two zones is a conduit, and the validator warns when one enters a higher security level through a zone with no appliance. Controllers, HMIs and historians are nodes of type `ot` (or `iot` for sensors and gateways) with `profile.platform.category: iot-ot`; the OT DMZ is a zone of kind `dmz` between the enterprise and the plant; vendor access is a `remote-user` on the Internet side through a `vpn` or `zero-trust-broker` appliance. Sources: IEC 62443-3-3 and 62443-4-2, ATT&CK for ICS (`attack.mitre_attack: ["T0886"]`), MITRE EMB3D for device-level threats, NIST SP 800-82. `scripts/suggest.mjs` raises IT traffic into an OT zone and the generic cases; the cards hold what is specific to control systems.

---

## `ot-controller` — Controller (PLC, RTU, DCS controller, safety controller)
- **Icon:** `ot` (node type `ot`)
- **What:** Runs the control logic that moves the process; speaks Modbus, DNP3, EtherNet/IP, PROFINET, S7 or OPC UA; often cannot authenticate anyone.
- **Attaches to:** The control zone (SL-T 2 or 3 typically); reached by the HMI and the engineering workstation; sometimes by the historian directly.
- **Default exposure:** Protocols with no authentication by design: whoever reaches the port can read and write; web and FTP services on the controller are common; firmware is rarely updated.
- **Disable unless needed:** **web-server-on-controller**, **ftp-or-telnet**, **program-mode-left-enabled**, **default-credentials**, **remote-program-download-from-non-engineering-hosts**, **snmp-v1-v2**.
- **Depends on:** `network` (the control zone and its conduit), `time`, the engineering workstation (`api`), `backup` (logic and configuration backups), `monitoring` (passive).
- **Ask:** Which protocols does it speak and do any authenticate? Which hosts may write to it (HMI, engineering workstation) and is that enforced by a firewall? Is the run/program key switch or mode protection used? Is the logic backed up and compared? Is there a safety system, and is it on a separate network?
- **Propose:** node `ot` in the control zone with `profile.platform.category: iot-ot` and `profile.patching`; `data` flows from the HMI and the engineering workstation with `auth: anonymous` (honestly) and `protocol` set; the zone's `security_level`; a firewall appliance on the control zone's edge that admits only those hosts.
- **Threats:** Unauthorised command message changes the process · Tampering · `broken-authentication` · CWE-306, T0855, T0836 · control: firewall admits writes only from the HMI and the engineering workstation, protocol-aware inspection where available, run-mode protection, OPC UA with security where supported ‖ Malicious logic downloaded to the controller · Tampering · `malware-execution` · CWE-494, T0843, T0821 · control: downloads only from the hardened engineering workstation with change control, logic backups compared on a schedule, controller mode locked ‖ Controller crashed by malformed traffic or a scan · Denial of service · `resource-exhaustion` · CWE-400, T0814, T0826 · control: no active scanning of control zones, rate limiting at the conduit, passive monitoring only, spare controllers and tested recovery.

## `ot-hmi-scada` — HMI and SCADA server (operator stations, supervisory servers)
- **Icon:** `ot` or `workstation` (node type `ot`, or `workstation` for an operator station)
- **What:** Where operators see and drive the process; a Windows host with vendor software, often old, often with shared accounts.
- **Attaches to:** The supervisory zone; talks to controllers in the control zone and to the historian in the DMZ.
- **Default exposure:** Shared operator accounts, auto-logon, no screen lock by design, unsupported Windows versions, USB open, remote desktop from the enterprise.
- **Disable unless needed:** **shared-operator-accounts-without-attribution**, **remote-desktop-from-enterprise**, **usb-storage**, **internet-or-mail-access**, **unsupported-os-without-isolation**, **vendor-default-credentials**.
- **Depends on:** `directory` (an OT domain, separate from the enterprise), `time`, the controllers (`api`), `backup`, `monitoring`, `update-service` (vendor-approved patches).
- **Ask:** Which OS and is it supported? How do operators sign in, and can actions be attributed? Can it reach or be reached from the enterprise network? Is application allow-listing on? How are vendor patches qualified and applied? Backups and tested restore?
- **Propose:** node `ot` ("SCADA server") and `workstation` nodes for operator stations in the supervisory zone with `profile` filled; `data` flows to the controllers; a `data` flow to the historian through the OT DMZ; the conduit's firewall as the zone appliance.
- **Threats:** Ransomware reaches the HMI from the enterprise network · Tampering · `malware-execution` · CWE-732, T0866, T0831 · control: no direct enterprise-to-supervisory path (OT DMZ only), application allow-listing, EDR where the vendor supports it, offline backups ‖ Shared operator account hides who changed a setpoint · Repudiation · `insider-misuse` · CWE-778, T0859 · control: named accounts or operator badges with the OT domain, HMI audit trail shipped to the OT SIEM ‖ Unsupported OS exploited through a known vulnerability · Elevation of privilege · `vulnerable-components` · CWE-1104, T0866 · control: upgrade on the vendor's path; until then isolate (no inbound but the conduit), allow-listing, compensating monitoring.

## `ot-historian` — Historian and OT DMZ data server
- **Icon:** `database` (node type `database`)
- **What:** Collects process data for the business; the one system both sides need, so the one that belongs in the OT DMZ.
- **Attaches to:** The OT DMZ (between the enterprise and the supervisory zone); collects from the SCADA or the controllers, serves the enterprise over its own protocol or a replica.
- **Default exposure:** A database and a web interface reachable from the enterprise; vendor service accounts with wide rights on both sides.
- **Disable unless needed:** **direct-enterprise-queries-to-the-control-zone**, **collector-with-write-rights-to-controllers**, **web-interface-without-authentication**, **shared-service-account-across-zones**.
- **Depends on:** `network` (the DMZ and its two conduits), `time`, `directory`, `storage`, `backup`, `logging`.
- **Ask:** Does the enterprise read a replica in the DMZ, or reach into the plant? Does the collector only read from the controllers? Which accounts cross the conduits and in which direction? Is the data flow one-way enforceable (data diode)?
- **Propose:** zone `dmz` ("OT DMZ") between the enterprise zone and the plant zones with firewalls on both edges; node `database` ("Historian") in it; a `data` flow from SCADA to the historian (initiated from the plant side where possible) and a `data` flow from enterprise users to the historian's replica.
- **Threats:** Historian used as the pivot from the enterprise into the control network · Elevation of privilege · `excessive-trust-or-flat-network` · CWE-923, T0886, T0866 · control: the enterprise reads a replica in the DMZ only, firewalls on both conduits with explicit rules, no shared accounts across zones, a data diode for one-way collection where possible ‖ Collector account with write access to controllers · Tampering · `privilege-escalation` · CWE-250, T0855 · control: read-only protocol access for the collector, write disabled at the controller and the firewall ‖ Process data altered to hide an event · Tampering · `insecure-design` · CWE-345, T0835 · control: write-once storage for raw history, integrity checks, replica compared with the source.

## `ot-engineering-workstation` — Engineering workstation
- **Icon:** `workstation` (node type `workstation`)
- **What:** The host that programs controllers and configures the HMI; the most powerful machine on the plant floor.
- **Attaches to:** The supervisory or control zone; often also the vendor's laptop plugged in when needed.
- **Default exposure:** Vendor software that needs local admin, project files with embedded credentials, USB used to move projects, Internet access "for licences".
- **Disable unless needed:** **internet-access**, **usb-project-transfer-without-scanning**, **vendor-laptops-on-the-control-network**, **local-admin-for-engineers**, **project-files-with-embedded-credentials**.
- **Depends on:** `directory` (OT domain), `backup` (projects), `update-service`, `monitoring`, the controllers (`api`).
- **Ask:** Who may use it and from where (plant floor only, or through the OT jump host)? Is the controller programming software the only path for logic downloads? Are projects in version control with backups? Is it hardened and allow-listed? How do vendors get their tools onto it?
- **Propose:** node `workstation` ("Engineering workstation") in the supervisory zone with `profile.elevated_access`; `data` flows to the controllers (`protocol` set, writes allowed only from here); a `data` flow from the OT jump host for remote engineering; a control for project version control.
- **Threats:** Compromised engineering workstation pushes logic to every controller · Tampering · `malware-execution` · CWE-494, T0843, T0873 · control: allow-listing, no Internet or mail, hardened and patched on the vendor's schedule, logic downloads under change control and compared ‖ Project files with embedded credentials copied around on USB · Information disclosure · `credential-exposure` · CWE-798, T0847 · control: projects in version control with secrets removed, USB blocked or scanned at a kiosk, vendor media quarantined ‖ Vendor laptop joins the control network · Elevation of privilege · `excessive-trust-or-flat-network` · CWE-923, T0848 · control: vendor work through the OT jump host or a dedicated, monitored engineering host; no foreign devices on control zones.

## `ot-dmz-jump` — OT DMZ and OT jump host
- **Icon:** `server` (node type `server`; zone kind `dmz`)
- **What:** The only way from the enterprise, or from a vendor, into the plant: a DMZ with a hardened jump host, brokered sessions and recording.
- **Attaches to:** Between the enterprise zone and the supervisory zone, with a firewall on each edge; vendor access terminates here.
- **Default exposure:** A jump host that doubles as a file server, remote desktop from the whole enterprise, vendor VPN that lands in the control zone directly.
- **Disable unless needed:** **remote-desktop-from-whole-enterprise**, **vendor-vpn-terminating-in-the-plant**, **file-shares-on-the-jump-host**, **standing-vendor-accounts**, **unrecorded-sessions**.
- **Depends on:** `directory` (OT domain), `identity` (MFA, the broker), `logging` (session recording), `time`, `update-service`.
- **Ask:** Is every remote session brokered through the jump host with MFA and recording? Are vendor accounts enabled only for the maintenance window? What can a session reach from the jump host (which controllers, which protocols)? Is file transfer separate and scanned?
- **Propose:** zone `dmz` ("OT DMZ") with firewalls on both edges; node `server` ("OT jump host") in it; `data` flows from enterprise administrators and vendors (`connection.type: remote-session`, strong `auth`) to the jump host, and from it to the engineering workstation or HMI; `profile.elevated_access` with session recording.
- **Threats:** Vendor remote access lands in the control zone unbrokered · Elevation of privilege · `excessive-trust-or-flat-network` · CWE-923, T0886, T0822 · control: every session through the DMZ jump host with MFA, time-boxed vendor accounts, recording, a firewall rule per session target ‖ Jump host becomes the pivot with files and credentials on it · Elevation of privilege · `credential-exposure` · CWE-522, T0859 · control: no file shares or saved credentials, Credential Guard or equivalent, rebuilt from image, EDR ‖ Unrecorded session on the plant · Repudiation · `insufficient-logging-monitoring` · CWE-778, T1070 · control: session recording to the OT SIEM, privileged command logging, review of every vendor session.

## `ot-safety-system` — Safety instrumented system (SIS, safety PLC)
- **Icon:** `ot` (node type `ot`)
- **What:** The system that brings the process to a safe state when the controls fail; its integrity is a matter of life and environment, not data.
- **Attaches to:** Its own zone (SL-T 3 or 4), ideally physically separate from the basic process control; connected to the control zone only for status, never for control.
- **Default exposure:** Integrated with the control system on the same network "for convenience"; the engineering software reaches it from the same workstation that programs the controllers.
- **Disable unless needed:** **safety-and-control-on-one-network**, **remote-engineering-of-the-sis**, **key-switch-left-in-program**, **status-link-that-allows-writes**.
- **Depends on:** `network` (its own zone and a one-way conduit), the engineering workstation (`api`) during commissioning only, `time`, `backup` (logic).
- **Ask:** Is the safety system on its own network, and is the link to the control system read-only? How is its logic changed, by whom, with what authorisation? Is the key switch used? Has the safety logic been backed up and compared since commissioning?
- **Propose:** zone `ot` ("Safety zone") with `security_level: 4` and a firewall or data diode as its appliance; node `ot` ("Safety controller"); a one-way `data` flow of status to the control zone; a `control` flow from the engineering workstation marked out of scope for normal operation or with `blocked_reason` when disabled.
- **Threats:** Safety logic altered to disable a trip · Tampering · `malware-execution` · CWE-494, T0843, T0880 · control: physically separate safety network, key switch in run, logic changes only on site with two-person authorisation and comparison ‖ Safety and control on one network: a control-system compromise reaches the safety system · Tampering · `excessive-trust-or-flat-network` · CWE-923, T0886 · control: separate zone with a one-way conduit, firewall or diode, no shared engineering path ‖ Spurious trip caused by network flooding · Denial of service · `resource-exhaustion` · CWE-400, T0814 · control: no active scanning or broadcast storms on the safety network, rate limiting at the conduit, monitoring passive.

## `ot-vendor-remote-access` — Vendor and remote maintenance access
- **Icon:** `remote-user` (node type `remote-user` on the Internet side)
- **What:** The equipment vendor, the integrator or the service company reaching the plant from outside for maintenance.
- **Attaches to:** The Internet side; through a `vpn` or `zero-trust-broker` appliance into the OT DMZ, never into the plant directly.
- **Default exposure:** A cellular modem on the controller cabinet, a vendor VPN with a shared account, TeamViewer on the HMI.
- **Disable unless needed:** **cellular-or-dial-in-modems-on-cabinets**, **vendor-managed-remote-tools-on-hmis**, **shared-vendor-accounts**, **always-on-vendor-vpn**, **vendor-access-to-safety-systems**.
- **Depends on:** `identity` (vendor identities with MFA), the OT DMZ jump host, `logging` (recording), `network` (the broker).
- **Ask:** Which vendors have access, to what, and how (VPN, broker, modem)? Are their accounts named, MFA-protected and enabled only during a window? Does every session go through the jump host and get recorded? Are there modems or remote tools the vendors installed themselves?
- **Propose:** node `remote-user` ("Vendor engineer", `zone: internet`); appliance `zero-trust-broker` or `vpn` on the enterprise or DMZ edge with `serves` naming the vendor; a `tunnel` or `data` flow to the OT jump host with `auth: mfa`; `blocked` flows for the direct paths that must not exist.
- **Threats:** Vendor credential reused from a breach opens the plant · Spoofing · `broken-authentication` · CWE-307, T0822, T0859 · control: named vendor identities with phishing-resistant MFA, time-boxed enablement, broker instead of VPN, session recording ‖ Forgotten modem or remote tool bypasses every control · Elevation of privilege · `security-misconfiguration` · CWE-284, T0883, T0822 · control: inventory of every remote path with an owner, modems removed, remote tools uninstalled from HMIs, passive monitoring that detects new paths ‖ Vendor session reaches more than the equipment under maintenance · Elevation of privilege · `excessive-trust-or-flat-network` · CWE-923, T0886 · control: per-session firewall rules from the jump host to the named targets only, no safety system access, review of the recording.

## `ot-protocol-gateway` — Protocol gateway and IIoT edge device
- **Icon:** `connector` (node type `iot` or `connector`)
- **What:** Translates between OT protocols and IT ones (Modbus to MQTT, OPC UA to cloud); often the first OT device with an Internet path.
- **Attaches to:** The edge of the control or supervisory zone with one foot on each side; publishes to a broker or a cloud IoT hub.
- **Default exposure:** A device with a web interface and default credentials, bridging two networks, with cloud credentials stored on it.
- **Disable unless needed:** **web-interface-on-the-ot-side**, **default-credentials**, **bidirectional-bridging-when-publish-only-would-do**, **cloud-credentials-in-plaintext**, **unsigned-firmware**.
- **Depends on:** `network` (both zones), `messaging` (the broker), `pki` (device certificates), `time`, `update-service`, `identity` (device identity in the cloud).
- **Ask:** Which direction does data flow, and can the cloud side write to the controllers? How does the device authenticate to the cloud (certificate, key, password)? Who administers it and from where? Is firmware signed and updated? Is it on an inventory with an owner?
- **Propose:** node `iot` ("Protocol gateway") on the zone edge; a `data` flow from the controllers (read) and a `data` flow to the broker or cloud hub with `auth: certificate` or `api-key` (honestly), `encrypted: true`; `blocked` flow from the cloud side to the controllers when writes are disabled; `profile.device_authentication`.
- **Threats:** Gateway compromised from the IT or cloud side writes to the controllers · Tampering · `excessive-trust-or-flat-network` · CWE-923, T0886, T0855 · control: publish-only configuration, writes disabled at the gateway and the controller, firewall between the gateway and the control zone ‖ Device credentials for the cloud stolen from the gateway · Spoofing · `credential-exposure` · CWE-798, T1552 · control: per-device certificates in a secure element, rotation, device identity revocable in the cloud ‖ Unsigned or outdated firmware exploited · Elevation of privilege · `vulnerable-components` · CWE-1395, T0866 · control: signed firmware only, updates on the vendor's schedule, inventory with an owner, web interface off or restricted to the management network.
